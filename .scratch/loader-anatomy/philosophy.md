# Temporal Dissection

A movement devoted to the belief that time can be laid flat. What unfolds in five seconds before a viewer's eye — a waking, a charging, a departure — can be pinned to paper like a specimen: every instant given a coordinate, every gesture assigned an index. Temporal Dissection treats sequence as sacred material. The frame is the atom; the interval between frames is the void that gives the atom meaning. Nothing moves on the canvas, yet everything is understood to have moved.

The visual language is that of the archive and the laboratory bench: hairline rules, specimen borders, numbered plates, measured rulers. Forms repeat with the patience of a metronome — the same figure returned to again and again, each iteration differing by a breath. This repetition is not redundancy but devotion: the meticulous craftsmanship of someone who has counted every fiftieth of a second and refuses to let a single one go undocumented. The work must read as the product of countless hours at the light table, master-level execution in every margin and tick mark.

Color is rationed like a reagent. A field of quiet paper; ink in one near-black; a single warm accent — the orange of a chair back, a coffee's surface, a laptop's glowing seal — deployed only where life enters the system. Status is expressed chromatically and nowhere else: the alarm of red, the patience of amber, the arrival of green. These small chromatic events are placed with painstaking attention, each one earned by the rhythm that precedes it.

Composition proceeds by cadence. A horizontal strip of plates establishes the metre; beneath it, a ruler translates duration into distance, letting the eye walk the timeline the way the animation walks the clock. Departure — the final upward vanishing — is not drawn but implied: an empty direction, an arrow, a notation, a curtain's absence. The philosophy holds that the most masterful stroke in any sequence is the one that leaves the page.

Typography is whispered and clinical: thin, monospaced, archival. Words appear only as labels, coordinates, and quiet measurements — never as explanation. The document should feel discovered rather than composed, as though an imaginary discipline had spent decades studying a five-second waking and this sheet were its definitive plate. Every hairline aligned, every number kerned, every plate spaced by hand: work that could hang in a museum of ephemeral things, undeniable in its craft.
